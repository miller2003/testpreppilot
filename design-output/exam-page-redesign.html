Quick Answer

The bottom line

To become a licensed real estate sales agent in Texas, you must be at least 18, complete 180 hours of TREC-approved qualifying education, pass a background check, and pass the Pearson VUE exam (125 questions, 70% on each section). Your license issues inactive — you then need a sponsoring broker to activate it. Most people finish in 3–6 months at a cost of roughly $570–$1,290.

No college degree required — just 180 hrs of approved courses + a high school diploma or GED.
Education is 100% online with most TREC-approved schools — study at your own pace.
You have up to 3 attempts to pass both exam sections before extra education is required.
First-time pass rate is about 56% — structured exam prep is the differentiator.

Eligibility & Requirements

Texas has some of the most structured licensing rules in the country. Before you spend a dollar on courses, make sure you qualify.

You must meet all of the following to apply for a Texas sales agent license:

Be at least 18 years old
Be a U.S. citizen or lawfully admitted alien (Texas residency is not required)
Hold a high school diploma or GED
Meet TREC's standards for honesty, trustworthiness & integrity
Complete 180 hours of TREC-approved qualifying education
Have a criminal history? File a Fitness Determination (Form FD-7) with TREC before you pay for any courses. TREC reviews offenses case-by-case, and getting clarity up front saves you money and disappointment.

The 180-hour education breakdown

Texas requires six 30-hour qualifying courses (180 hours total). You may take them in any order, but most students start with Principles I & II. Course completion certificates are valid for one year.

Required course Hours What it covers
Principles of Real Estate I 30 Foundations of real estate, property ownership, land use, and the sales agent's role
Principles of Real Estate II 30 Licensing law, ethics, finance fundamentals, and Texas-specific principles
Law of Agency 30 Fiduciary duties, creating and terminating agency, buyer/seller representation
Law of Contracts 30 Contract formation, interpretation, breach, and remedies
Promulgated Contract Forms 30 Correct use of TREC's state-approved forms; avoiding unauthorized practice of law
Real Estate Finance 30 Lending basics, mortgage types, financing laws, and buyer options

The Exam in Detail

The Texas Real Estate Sales Agent Exam is administered by Pearson VUE at a physical testing center — it is not available online. It's computer-based, multiple-choice, and split into two graded sections.

Section Questions Time limit Passing score
National 85 150 min 56 correct (70%)
State (Texas) 40 90 min 21 correct (70%)
Total 125 240 min (4 hrs) Both sections required
Each section is scored independently. You must hit 70% on both the national and state portions. If you fail only one, you retake that section only — and you get up to three attempts to pass both before TREC requires additional education.

What's actually on the test

The national portion tests general real estate principles used across the U.S.; the state portion is laser-focused on Texas law. Exact topic weighting is published in the official Pearson VUE Candidate Handbook — these are the content areas you'll be tested on:

National content areas

  • Property ownership & land use controls
  • Agency law & fiduciary duties
  • Valuation & market analysis
  • Financing & lending fundamentals
  • Contracts — formation, enforceability, breach
  • Transfer of title, deeds & closing
  • Disclosures, leasing & property management
  • Practice & ethics (fair housing) + real estate math

Texas-specific topics

  • Texas Real Estate License Act (TRELA)
  • TREC rules & commission powers
  • Promulgated contract forms & proper use
  • Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA)
  • Intermediary relationships & IABS
  • Community property & homestead
  • Trust accounts & escrow handling
  • Consumer protection & required disclosures

Your Step-by-Step Route

Here's the process in the order TREC expects, with the practical tips that aren't written in the statute.

1
Eligibility Confirm you qualify Verify you're 18+, a U.S. citizen or lawful resident, hold a high school diploma/GED, and can meet TREC's integrity standards. If you have any criminal history, resolve a Fitness Determination (FD-7) first.
Don't buy courses until your Fitness Determination clears — certificates are only valid for one year, and a denied application means money spent in vain.
2
Education Complete 180 hours of qualifying education Finish all six 30-hour TREC-approved courses from a single approved provider (or mix providers). Your completion certificates are filed with TREC and remain valid for 12 months.
Self-paced online study is the norm. Budget exam-prep time on top of the 180 hours — the first-time pass rate is only ~56%.
3
Application Apply to TREC & get fingerprinted Submit your sales agent application through TREC's online portal and pay the application fee. Then schedule fingerprints with IdentoGO (by IDEMIA) using your TREC ID. Results go straight to TREC.
The application fee recently shifted (roughly $185–$206 depending on the effective schedule). Check TREC's current fee table before paying — it changes periodically.
4
Exam Schedule & pass the Pearson VUE exam Once TREC issues your eligibility letter, book the exam at a Pearson VUE center. You'll have 240 minutes for 125 questions and must score 70% on both the national and state sections.
Fail one section? Retake just that one. You get up to three attempts before TREC requires more education. The $43 fee applies per attempt.
5
Sponsorship Find a sponsoring broker Pass and TREC issues an inactive license. A licensed Texas broker must file a sponsorship request in TREC's system to activate it — without this, you cannot legally practice or earn commissions.
Interview 2–3 brokerages. Compare commission splits, training, and lead sources — not just the highest split. The right mentor matters more early on.
6
Maintain Activate, then keep it current Once sponsored, your license is active. To renew (every 2 years), complete continuing education: your first renewal requires 90 hours of SAE plus Legal Update I & II; subsequent cycles need 18 hours of CE.
Mark your renewal date now. TREC sends reminders ~90 days out, but a lapsed license means inactive status and late fees.

State FAQ

Do I need a college degree to get a Texas real estate license?
No. A degree isn't required — only a high school diploma or GED plus the 180 hours of approved qualifying education. Many successful agents start straight out of high school or a different career.
Can I take the Texas real estate exam online?
No. The exam is administered in person at a Pearson VUE testing center only. You'll take it on a computer at the center — there is no remote or online proctored option.
What score do I need to pass?
You need 70% on each section independently: at least 56 of 85 on the national portion and 21 of 40 on the state portion. Passing one and failing the other means you retake only the failed section.
How long does the background check take?
Fingerprint results typically return in a couple of weeks, but TREC's full review can take longer if you have a criminal record or owe judgments. Filing a Fitness Determination early keeps things moving.
How much does it cost to get licensed in Texas?
Plan for roughly $570–$1,290 all-in. Fixed state fees (application, fingerprinting, exam) are about $270; the big variable is education tuition, which ranges from ~$300 to ~$1,000 depending on the school and package.
How long does the whole process take?
Most candidates finish in 3–6 months. Fast tracks can do it in under two months if you study full-time; slower self-paced routes stretch toward six.
Do I need a sponsoring broker before I take the exam?
No — you can take and pass the exam without a broker. But your license issues inactive, and you can't practice or earn commissions until a broker sponsors you in TREC's system.
Can I complete the education online?
Yes. As long as the provider is TREC-approved, every required hour can be completed online. Most schools are fully self-paced with mobile access.
What's the first-time pass rate?
About 56% of first-time candidates passed as of late 2025. The exam is challenging by design — pairing your 180 hours with a dedicated Texas exam-prep course is the most reliable way to beat the average.
Do I have to live in Texas to get a Texas license?
No residency is required — you only need to be a U.S. citizen or lawfully admitted alien. That said, you'll be practicing Texas law, so most out-of-state agents partner with a Texas brokerage.
How do I keep my license active after I pass?
Licenses renew every 2 years. Your first renewal requires 90 hours of SAE plus Legal Update I & II; later cycles need 18 hours of continuing education. TREC sends reminders about 90 days before expiration.
