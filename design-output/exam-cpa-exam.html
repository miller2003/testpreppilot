Certified Public Accountant: Requirements, Exam & Step-by-Step Path

The CPA license is the highest standard of competence in the field of accountancy. It requires meeting the 3 Es: Education (150 hours), Exam (4 parts), and Experience (1 year).

Quick Answer

The bottom line

The CPA license is earned in three stages — Education (150 credit hours), Exam (4 sections under the CPA Evolution model), and Experience (typically 1–2 years). You apply through your State Board of Accountancy, receive a Notice to Schedule (NTS), and sit for the Core and Discipline sections at a Prometric center. Most candidates finish in 12–18 months.

Highest recognized credential in accounting — the “gold standard”
Required (by law) to sign audit opinions and issue attestations
Opens audit, tax, consulting, and CFO-track career paths
Strong, durable salary premium over non-CPA accountants
Portable across states through reciprocity once licensed

The Exam in Detail

Under CPA Evolution (2024+), the exam has 3 mandatory Core sections and 1 Discipline you choose. Each section is 4 hours, mixing multiple-choice questions with task-based simulations (and a written task on some). A scaled 75 of 99 passes:

  • Audit procedures & reporting

  • Conceptual framework & GAAP
  • Financial statements & disclosures
  • Governmental & non-profit accounting
  • Selected transactions & reporting

Core — REG (Regulation)

  • Entity taxation (C-corp, S-corp, partnership)

  • Data governance & lifecycle
  • IT security & controls

Your Step-by-Step Route

Here is the process in the order CPA candidates actually follow — with the practical tips that aren’t written in the statute.

1
Step 1 Meet the 150-hour education requirement Every U.S. jurisdiction requires 150 semester hours of college education to sit — 30 hours beyond a typical bachelor’s degree. If you’re short, add graduate courses, community-college classes, or a master’s.
  • Bachelor’s degree (≈120 hrs)
  • Additional 30 hrs (graduate, community college, or online)
Some states let you sit with 120 hours but require 150 to actually license.
2
Step 2 Apply to your State Board of Accountancy Submit your application, official transcripts, and the application fee ($20–$150 by state). The Board verifies your credits and issues an Attestation of Eligibility (ATT).
  • Send official transcripts
  • Pay the Board application fee
  • Wait for ATT (valid 90 days)
2–6 weeks
3
Step 3 Receive your Notice to Schedule (NTS) Once approved, pay the NASBA exam fee (~$262.64 per section) and receive your NTS, valid for 6 months. You need the NTS to book a Prometric appointment.
  • Pay NASBA the per-section fee
  • Receive NTS (valid 6 months)
  • Book Prometric appointment
International candidates pay an extra $375–$390 per section.
4
Step 4 Pass the 4 exam sections Under CPA Evolution the exam has 3 mandatory Core sections — AUD (Audit), FAR (Financial Accounting & Reporting), REG (Regulation) — plus 1 Discipline you choose: BAR (Business Analysis & Reporting), ISC (Information Systems & Controls), or TCP (Tax Compliance & Planning). Each is 4 hours, with MCQs plus task-based simulations, scaled 0–99 (pass 75).
  • Core: AUD
  • Core: FAR
  • Core: REG
  • Discipline: BAR / ISC / TCP (choose one)
6–12 months of testing
All 4 must be passed within an 18-month (most states) or 30-month window.
5
Step 5 Pass the AICPA Ethics Exam Most states require the AICPA self-study Ethics Exam — a 40-question, open-book test — before licensing. Cost is typically $150–$200. A few weeks
6
Step 6 Complete the experience requirement Nearly every state requires 1–2 years (about 2,000 hours) of supervised accounting experience, verified by a licensed CPA.
  • Secure a CPA-supervised role
  • Log ~2,000 hours
  • Obtain CPA verification
1–2 years
Some states accept industry or non-public accounting experience.
7
Step 7 Apply for your license Submit your final application and pay the license fee ($100–$500). Once issued, maintain it with ~120 hours of CPE every 3 years (varies by state). 2–4 weeks

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the CPA Evolution?
A new CPA exam model introduced in 2024 featuring 3 mandatory Core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) and 1 of 3 chosen Discipline sections (BAR, ISC, TCP).
How many exam sections are there and what are they?
Four: the three Core sections — AUD, FAR, REG — and one Discipline you pick: BAR, ISC, or TCP.
What score do I need to pass?
A scaled 75 on the 0–99 exam scale, per section. It is a scaled score, not the percentage of questions you answered correctly.
How long is the CPA exam and how long does the whole process take?
Each section is 4 hours. Start to finish, most candidates license in 12–18 months including education, testing, ethics, and experience.
What is the 150-hour rule?
Every U.S. jurisdiction requires 150 semester hours of college credit to sit — about 30 hours beyond a typical bachelor’s degree.
How much does it cost?
Plan for $1,400–$2,000+ in fees (Board application, ~$262.64/section NASBA fee, Ethics, license). A review course adds $1,500–$3,500.
CPA vs Enrolled Agent — which should I pursue?
The CPA is state-licensed with the broadest scope, including audit and attest, and needs 150 hours. The EA is IRS-licensed for federal tax only, needs no degree, and is faster and cheaper.
Can I take the CPA exam without an accounting degree?
You need 150 credit hours, but not necessarily an accounting degree — many candidates reach 150 with a non-accounting bachelor’s plus extra coursework.
What is the 18-month window?
Once you pass your first section, most states give you 18 months (some 30) to pass the remaining three before the first expires.
How hard is the CPA exam and what are the pass rates?
It is widely regarded as difficult. Recent pass rates run roughly AUD 48%, FAR 42%, REG 63%, BAR 42%, ISC 68%, and TCP 78%.
