Quick Answer

The bottom line

To become a California real estate salesperson you must be 18+, complete 135 hours of DRE-approved education (Principles + Practice + one elective), pass a background check via Live Scan, and pass the 150-question state exam at 70%. You then find a sponsoring broker — your license is issued but stays inactive until a broker signs on. Most people finish in 2–6 months at a total cost of about $600–$1,200.

No college degree required — only three 45-hour courses
One of the largest, highest-value markets in the U.S.
License is portable within California's huge economy
Strong commission potential for productive agents

Your Step-by-Step Route

Here is the process in the order CA Real Estate candidates actually follow — with the practical tips that aren’t written in the statute.

1
Step 1 Confirm you meet DRE eligibility You must be at least 18, provide proof of legal presence, and have an SSN or ITIN. No degree is required, but you must disclose any criminal history — most non-violent offenses are reviewed case-by-case.
  • 18+ years old
  • Proof of legal presence
  • SSN or ITIN
2
Step 2 Complete 135 hours of approved education Three 45-hour DRE-approved courses: Real Estate Principles (required), Real Estate Practice (required, now includes implicit-bias and fair-housing training), and one 45-hour elective (e.g., Legal Aspects, Real Estate Finance, Appraisal, Economics, Property Management). DRE pacing rules require a minimum of 18 days per course (54 days total).
  • Real Estate Principles (45 hrs)
  • Real Estate Practice (45 hrs)
  • One elective (45 hrs)
4–8 weeks typical
Members of the California State Bar are exempt from the courses.
3
Step 3 Apply for the exam and submit fingerprints File the Combined Exam + License Application (RE 435) through the DRE eLicensing portal and pay the fees (exam $100, original license $350). Complete Live Scan fingerprinting (DOJ + FBI) for the background check — you can do this before or after the exam.
  • Submit RE 435 (eLicensing)
  • Pay $100 exam + $350 license
  • Live Scan fingerprinting (~$49–$90)
2–4 weeks processing
If you do not apply for the license within 2 years of passing, you must retake the exam.
4
Step 4 Schedule and pass the state exam Once approved, schedule the exam at a DRE/PSI testing center. It has 150 multiple-choice questions and a 3-hour time limit; a score of 70% (105/150) passes. Results are given immediately.
  • 150 multiple-choice questions
  • 3-hour limit
  • Passing score 70%
Same day
If you fail, wait at least 18 days and repay the $100 exam fee to retake; no limit on attempts.
5
Step 5 Find a sponsoring broker to activate Your salesperson license is issued but cannot be used until a licensed California broker sponsors you. Interview brokerages, compare splits and training, and have the broker countersign your license.
You cannot practice real estate in California without a sponsoring broker.

State FAQ

Do I need a college degree to get a California real estate license?
No. You only need three 45-hour DRE-approved courses (Principles, Practice, and one elective) plus a high-school diploma or equivalent.
Can I take the California real estate exam online?
No. The exam is administered in person at a DRE/PSI testing center only.
What score do I need to pass?
70%, which is 105 of the 150 questions. Results are available the moment you finish.
How long is the California exam?
Three hours for 150 multiple-choice questions.
How much does it cost to get licensed in California?
Plan for roughly $600–$1,200 all-in: $100 exam fee, $350 original license fee, Live Scan fingerprints (~$49–$90), and a 135-hour course ($100–$700).
How long does the whole process take?
Most people finish in 2–6 months, depending on study pace and how fast DRE processes the file.
Do I need a sponsoring broker before the exam?
No — you can take and pass the exam first. But your license issues inactive until a California broker sponsors you.
Is California real estate education available online?
Yes. As long as the provider is DRE-approved, all 135 hours can be completed online at your own pace.
What is the first-time pass rate?
About 51% in recent years — California is one of the harder salesperson exams, so dedicated exam prep matters.
Does California have reciprocity with other states?
No. Out-of-state licensees must still pass the California exam; there is no reciprocal license.
