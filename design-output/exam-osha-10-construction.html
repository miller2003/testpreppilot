Quick Answer

The bottom line

OSHA 10 Construction is a 10-hour, entry-level outreach course that gives workers baseline awareness of the most common job-site hazards — falls, electrocution, struck-by, caught-in/between, PPE, and more. It ends with a DOL wallet card that many contractors require for site access. You can finish online in a day or two; there is no formal exam to fail, and the card never expires under federal rules (though employers often want a refresher every 3–5 years).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the purpose of OSHA 10 Construction?
It is entry-level hazard-awareness training that helps workers recognize and avoid the leading job-site dangers — falls, electrocution, struck-by, and caught-in/between.
Can I take OSHA 10 online?
Yes. OSHA-authorized trainers offer the full 10-hour course 100% online; you can also take it in person.
How long does it take?
The 10 required hours are spread over at least two days (7.5-hour daily cap); most finish in 1–2 days of focused study.
Does the OSHA 10 card expire?
Under federal OSHA the card never expires, but many employers and some jurisdictions require a refresher every 3–5 years.
How much does it cost?
Typically $25–$89 online, with the official DOL card included. In-person courses run higher.
OSHA 10 vs OSHA 30 — which do I need?
OSHA 10 is for entry-level workers; OSHA 30 is for supervisors and anyone with safety responsibility. You can take 30 directly without 10.
Is OSHA 10 required by law?
Federal OSHA does not require it, but several states and cities mandate it for site access, and many contractors require it as a condition of employment.
Is the DOL card the same as a certificate?
The certificate proves completion; the official DOL wallet card is the industry-standard proof accepted on job sites.
