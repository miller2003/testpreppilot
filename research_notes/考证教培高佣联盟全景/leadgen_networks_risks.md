# Education Lead-Gen Networks, Multi-Merchant Affiliate Networks, and Approval/Compliance Risks for testpreppilot.com (as of 2026-10-09)

Scope note: about 22 search/fetch calls. Several primary pages (FTC.gov) failed to load (socket errors), so some legal points rely on law-firm summaries; these are flagged. All money figures are USD unless noted. Publisher payout numbers are scarce publicly. Most lead-gen networks negotiate payouts privately or show them only after login, so many items below are **gaps**, not numbers.

## (A) Lead gen: which networks pay publishers for education leads, and what are typical CPLs?

### Takeaway
The big education lead aggregators (QuinStreet Education, now EducationDynamics, plus DMS, Education Connection and ClassesUSA) are buy-side or enterprise operations. None has a public, self-serve publisher program with posted CPLs. Public publisher-side education CPLs found online are low ($1-$25 per lead) and mostly stale. Advertiser-side CPLs (what schools pay) run about $35-$200, and up to about $1,000 for specialized programs, but a publisher only keeps part of that. A new, low-traffic pSEO site is unlikely to get direct access to high-CPL school leads. The practical route is CPA networks (MaxBounty, FlexOffers) with posted per-offer rates, plus the TCPA consent burden that comes with any lead form.

### Cited Findings

The sale closed Aug 31, 2020, and QuinStreet now focuses on financial and home services. **So "QuinStreet Education" no longer exists as a publisher program** — [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/5077/educationdynamics-acquires-quinstreets-higher-education-vertical/); [ROI-NJ 2020-09-03](https://www.roi-nj.com/2020/09/03/uncategorized/higher-ed-services-firm-acquires-digital-asset-vertical/); [H.I.G./WhiteHorse financing note 2020-09-30](https://hig.com/news/whitehorse-capital-provides-incremental-term-loan-to-educationdynamics-llc-to-support-acquisition-of-quinstreet-education)

A 2003 DeVry contract set a per-lead fee (stale) — [QuinStreet investor filing](https://investor.quinstreet.com/static-files/7430a680-1904-449e-bdf7-495533ce1b0d); [Justia contract](https://contracts.justia.com/companies/quinstreet-inc-1099/contract/694312)

**Advertiser-side CPL benchmarks (what schools pay; USD; vendor/agency blogs, directional)**
- Higher-ed PPL: about $140-$200 for most programs, online certificates $50-$100, and specialized healthcare programs "can exceed $1,000." PPL leads convert at about 2% vs 4-5% for first-party leads, about 50% "go dark," and PPL drives about 4% of enrollments (down from more than 8%) — [Launchcodex](https://launchcodex.com/blog/industry-news-shifts/higher-education-lead-generation-pay-per-lead-strategies/)
- About $200 for typical institutions and up to $1,000 for specialized ones — [Level Agency 2025](https://www.level.agency/?p=6888)
- 2026 benchmarks: cost per inquiry $128 for undergraduate and $157 for graduate. Cost per enrolled student is $1,505 for undergraduate and $3,804 for graduate — [Foundry CRO 2026](https://foundrycro.com/blog/higher-education-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)
- Trade schools: $35-$120 per lead (typical $55-$85, premium above $120). Inquiry-to-enrollment ranges from 1-in-4 to 1-in-12 depending on the program — [NN Partners](https://nn.partners/blog/trade-school-cost-per-enrollment/)
- CDL: one agency case study (Abylex truck school) claims 1,400+ leads, 865 enrollments in 9 months, and about $55 per acquisition, vs a prior $150-$200 per conversion. This is self-reported agency marketing — [LSEO](https://lseo.com/our-work/ablyex-case-study/)

**TCPA / consent for lead forms**
- The FCC "one-to-one consent" rule (consent limited to a single seller, plus "logically and topically related") was vacated by the 11th Circuit in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. FCC*, No. 24-10277, on Jan 24, 2025. The FCC formally removed it from 47 CFR 64.1200(f) by a final rule around Sept 2025. Consent is still required under the TCPA "prior express consent" standard (prior express *written* consent for telemarketing robocalls and robotexts), and recordkeeping is advised — [Morrison Foerster](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Goodwin 2025-09](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2025/09/the-fcc-issues-final-rule-formally-eliminating-the-one-to-one-consent-requirement); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule)

### Inferences
- In 2026, "education lead gen" is consolidated (EducationDynamics) or restructured (DMS). The high CPLs schools pay ($100-$1,000) go mostly to these aggregators and their established publisher networks, not to small new sites. A site with about 1,500 impressions per day and few clicks would generate very few form fills even at a 9% lead rate.
- Hosting your own school-matching lead form brings TCPA consent-language, consent-logging (e.g., TrustedForm/Jornaya-type certificates) and possibly state mini-TCPA (FL/OK/MD) obligations. Simpler route: link out to a network-hosted offer page where the buyer captures consent. This is an inference; the state-law angle was not researched here.
- The test-prep audience (people studying for a specific exam) overlaps only partly with "find a school" lead buyers. The strongest overlap is in licensing paths that require a pre-licensing school (contractor, real estate, insurance, CDL, CNA/LPN, cosmetology). Those are typically CPS affiliate programs, not CPL.

### Gaps
- No current, verifiable publisher CPLs for EducationDynamics, Education Connection, Campus Explorer, Edupath, EDDY/EducationDirectory, Cygnus Education, Perform[cb] education, CPAlead or ClickBank education. Nothing public was found, and these terms are typically login-gated or negotiated.
- UTI / Lincoln Tech referral payouts and CDL-recruitment lead networks (Drive My Way, etc.) were not found publicly. Sallie Mae's affiliate program was not researched (lending, not education leads).
- MaxBounty's live education CPLs need a logged-in check.

## (B) Affiliate networks: test-prep/certification merchant coverage, and can Skimlinks/Sovrn cover everything?

### Takeaway
Test-prep and certification merchants are spread across Impact (Coursera, Magoosh), Awin, which absorbed ShareASale (UWorld, Princeton Review), PartnerStack (edX) and FlexOffers (which sub-lists Kaplan, Princeton Review and Magoosh). Typical rates are 5-15% CPS, with Coursera up to 45%. Skimlinks or Sovrn can auto-monetize links to many of these with one account, at about a 25% cut of commission (third-party reported) and about 90-day payment lag. They cannot reach merchants that aren't on networks they aggregate, and they don't do CPL education offers.

### Cited Findings
- **ShareASale is gone.** Awin migrated all active ShareASale customers by Aug 15, 2025. The platform and account access shut down Oct 6, 2025 (historical reports unavailable afterward), and shareasale.com forwards to Awin — [Hello Partner](https://hellopartner.com/shareasale-to-sunset-in-us-as-awin-migration-wraps-up/); [Awin announcement](https://awin.com/us/news-and-events/awin-news/awin-shareasale-new-era)
- **Coursera** (Impact): baseline 15%-45% commission on courses, Specializations, Professional Certificates and Coursera Plus; 30-day window; covers 4,000+ courses. Third parties conflict on whether degrees and certificates are excluded and say subscriptions pay the first month only — [Coursera affiliates page](https://coursera.org/about/affiliates); [Referly](https://www.referly.so/affiliate-programs/coursera)
- **Magoosh** (Impact): content partners "typically start at 10%," coupon/loyalty partners about 1%, 30-day attribution. Third-party listings show 8% — [Magoosh partner page](https://magoosh.com/partner-with-us); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/magoosh-affiliate-program/)
- **UWorld** (Awin): 10% default on Accounting, Finance and College Prep categories, 30-day cookie — [Awin merchant profile 120129](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/120129)
- **The Princeton Review** (Awin): up to 6%, **10-day cookie**, 270-day locking period. Publishers must generate clicks within 45 days of approval or be removed. Other listings conflict: 8% (Lasso), 5% (FlexOffers), 3% (Shopper.com) — [Awin profile 18466](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/18466); [Awin terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/18466); [Lasso](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/princeton-review/); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/the-princeton-review-affiliate-program)
- **Kaplan** (FlexOffers listings): 12%, 8% and 5% of sales in different snapshots (conflicting or stale), 30-day cookie, and an average purchase above $1,200 — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/kaplan-test-prep-affiliate-program/); [Affilimate](https://affilimate.io/programs/kaplan-affiliate-program/)
- **edX** (PartnerStack): 5-10% per sale, $50 threshold. **Udacity**: 24% of the enrollment fee, 30-day cookie. **Outschool**: 10% — [Lasso education list](https://getlasso.co/niche/education/)
- **Skimlinks**: claims about 48,500 merchants across 50+ networks. Free to join. The standard split is widely reported as 75% publisher / 25% Skimlinks of the *commission*, but Skimlinks' own FAQ only says it takes "a small percentage." Merchants take about 60 days to validate, about 90 days to payout. Third-party listing shows a $65 minimum — [Skimlinks FAQ](https://www.skimlinks.com/insights/faq-skimlinks-payments/); [UpPromote directory](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/skimlinks/); [MakeInfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/skimlinks-viglink-sovrn-commerce-and-automatic-affiliate-link-conversion)
- **Sovrn Commerce** (ex-VigLink): its revenue share is not publicly disclosed. It has CPA and CPC merchants, and since July 2024 "Unified Optimization" routes clicks to the highest-EPC network. About 90-day payment lag (unverified third party) — [Referly](https://www.referly.so/affiliate-programs/sovrn-commerce); [StreetInsider/PRNewswire](https://www.streetinsider.com/PRNewswire/SOVRN+BRINGS+ITS+PROGRAMMATIC+EXPERTISE+TO+THE+AFFILIATE+SPACE+WITH+UNIFIED+OPTIMIZATION/23515429.html)

### Inferences
- Through Skimlinks, a 10% test-prep merchant pays about 7.5% to the publisher (at the reported 75/25 split). That is coverage at the cost of about a quarter of revenue, plus whatever merchants block sub-affiliates.
- Coverage gaps likely remain for official exam bodies (CompTIA, Pearson VUE, PMI, NCLEX/NCSBN, state licensing boards), which generally don't run affiliate programs. No evidence of such programs was found, so this is unconfirmed.
- Cheapest broad-coverage stack (inference): Impact (Coursera, Magoosh) + Awin (UWorld, Princeton Review, ex-ShareASale) + FlexOffers (aggregator listing Kaplan and others) + Skimlinks as the catch-all.

### Gaps
- No education-specific Skimlinks or Sovrn merchant-rate lists were found. Whether Kaplan, UWorld and Princeton Review allow sub-network traffic is unknown.
- CJ, Rakuten, Partnerize, Admitad and Refersion test-prep merchant counts were not established. No CompTIA, Pearson VUE or Google Career Certificate affiliate terms were found.
- No source for "commission data staleness" quantified beyond the conflicting Kaplan and Princeton Review listings above.

## (C) Approval and compliance risks for a new AI-assisted pSEO site

### Takeaway
The main risks are (1) network and merchant rejection for being new, low-traffic or thin; (2) Google's **scaled content abuse** policy, which explicitly names "using generative AI tools ... to generate many pages without adding value"; (3) FTC disclosure rules, which require a clear disclosure near each affiliate link, not just a footer; and (4) TCPA obligations if the site collects leads. One-to-one consent is no longer required, but prior express written consent still is.

### Cited Findings
**Approval**
- AvantLink does not accept sites that are "brand new, or have only recently been registered (0-4 months as a general rule)" — [AvantLink](https://www.avantlink.com/top-5-reasons-your-affiliate-application-was-denied/)
- Vendor-reported traffic thresholds of 1,000-10,000 or 5,000-10,000 monthly visitors (unverified, vendor blogs). Thin content (fewer than 10 posts) is a common decline reason. No source says networks reject AI content *per se*; the concern is low-value content — [QualityUnit](https://cdn.qualityunit.com/faq/why-do-affiliate-networks-reject-applications); [Senja](https://support.senja.io/why-was-my-affiliate-application-declined-common-reasons-and-how-to-fix-them-adxpa); [AffNinja](https://affninja.com/affiliate-network-approval-statistics/)
- Merchant-level activity requirements: Princeton Review on Awin removes publishers that generate no clicks within 45 days of approval — [Awin terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/18466)

**Google Search policy** (Search Central spam policies, last updated 2026-08-28)
- "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users." The examples include "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" and "Stitching or combining content from different web pages without adding value" — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- "Thin affiliation" means pages with affiliate links where descriptions or reviews are copied from the merchant "without any original content or added value." Google points to original reviews, comparisons and pricing info as signs of a good affiliate page — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- The site reputation abuse policy (Nov 19, 2024 update) targets third-party content hosted to exploit a host's ranking signals. "Affiliate links marked appropriately aren't considered site reputation abuse." It is mostly irrelevant to a first-party pSEO site, but led to manual actions on Forbes Advisor and CNN Underscored — [Google blog 2024-11](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse?hl=en); [Affiliate Summit](https://affiliatesummit.com/blogs/google-cracks-down-sites-violating-site-reputation-abuse-policy)

**FTC disclosure** (16 CFR Part 255, revised June 2023; FTC.gov pages failed to load, so these come from law-firm summaries)
- New Example 11 in §255.5(b): a blogger paid per sale through review links should "clearly and conspicuously disclose the compensation." Per the FAQs, a bare "affiliate link" or "buy now" button is inadequate, and "commissionable link" is not clear. "Paid link" next to the link can suffice if the review and its disclosure are visible together. Advertisers can be liable for deceptive endorsements (§255.1(d)) — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/consumer-law/1349688/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-to-the-endorsement-guidespart-2-of-2); [AvantLink FTC guidelines](https://support.avantlink.com/hc/en-us/articles/211635666-FTC-Guidelines-for-Affiliate-Marketing)

**TCPA**: see section (A). The one-to-one rule was vacated Jan 24, 2025 and removed by the FCC around Sept 2025 — [MoFo](https://mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/2025/09/the-fcc-issues-final-rule-formally-eliminating-the-one-to-one-consent-requirement)

**Cookie durations observed**: 10 days (Princeton Review/Awin) to 30 days (Coursera, Magoosh, UWorld, Kaplan). The historical 120-day CCI lead cookie shows how much windows have shrunk, but this is anecdotal — sources as cited above.

### Inferences
- testpreppilot.com (about 477 AI-assisted pages, new domain, few clicks) matches the profile networks reject: new, low traffic, templated. Expect approval friction at CJ, Awin, Rakuten and AvantLink-type networks. FlexOffers, Skimlinks and Impact marketplace brands may be easier, but this is not verified.
- An affiliate-heavy rollout across all 477 pages raises scaled-content and thin-affiliation risk. Adding unique value (exam-specific pass-rate data, original comparisons, real pricing) is Google's stated mitigation.
- Disclosure should appear near the first affiliate link on each guide page, not only in a site-wide footer.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found on trademark-bidding restrictions for these specific merchants (typically in each program's terms on Awin and Impact). Not verified here.
- Reddit or forum first-hand approval experiences for AI/pSEO sites in 2025-2026 were not found in this pass.
- The FTC.gov primary FAQ text could not be fetched (socket error), so it should be verified directly.
- No data on how Google treated AI pSEO affiliate sites in the 2025-2026 core and spam updates specifically.
