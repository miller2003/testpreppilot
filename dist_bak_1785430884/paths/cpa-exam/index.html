Certified Public Accountant

The CPA license is the highest standard of competence in the field of accountancy. It requires meeting the 3 Es: Education (150 hours), Exam (4 parts), and Experience (1 year).

Education Required 150 Credit Hours
Exam Sections 4
Quick Answer

The CPA license is earned in three stages — Education (150 credit hours), Exam (4 sections under the CPA Evolution model), and Experience (typically 1–2 years). You apply through your State Board of Accountancy, receive a Notice to Schedule (NTS), and sit for the Core and Discipline sections at a Prometric center. Most candidates finish in 12–18 months.

  • Highest recognized credential in accounting — the “gold standard”
  • Required (by law) to sign audit opinions and issue attestations
  • Opens audit, tax, consulting, and CFO-track career paths
  • Strong, durable salary premium over non-CPA accountants
  • Portable across states through reciprocity once licensed

The Path at a Glance

  1. Complete 150 college credit hours (bachelor’s + extra)
  2. Apply to your State Board of Accountancy
  3. Receive ATT & Notice to Schedule (NTS)
  4. Pass 3 Core + 1 Discipline sections within 18 or 30 months
  5. Pass the AICPA Ethics Exam (most states)
  6. Complete 1–2 years of supervised experience
  7. Apply for and receive your license

Your Route

  1. 1

    Meet the 150-hour education requirement

    Every U.S. jurisdiction requires 150 semester hours of college education to sit — 30 hours beyond a typical bachelor’s degree. If you’re short, add graduate courses, community-college classes, or a master’s.

    • Bachelor’s degree (≈120 hrs)
    • Additional 30 hrs (graduate, community college, or online)
    Note: Some states let you sit with 120 hours but require 150 to actually license.
  2. 2

    Apply to your State Board of Accountancy

    Submit your application, official transcripts, and the application fee ($20–$150 by state). The Board verifies your credits and issues an Attestation of Eligibility (ATT).

    • Send official transcripts
    • Pay the Board application fee
    • Wait for ATT (valid 90 days)
    ⏱ 2–6 weeks
  3. 3

    Receive your Notice to Schedule (NTS)

    Once approved, pay the NASBA exam fee (~$262.64 per section) and receive your NTS, valid for 6 months. You need the NTS to book a Prometric appointment.

    • Pay NASBA the per-section fee
    • Receive NTS (valid 6 months)
    • Book Prometric appointment
    Note: International candidates pay an extra $375–$390 per section.
  4. 4

    Pass the 4 exam sections

    Under CPA Evolution the exam has 3 mandatory Core sections — AUD (Audit), FAR (Financial Accounting & Reporting), REG (Regulation) — plus 1 Discipline you choose: BAR (Business Analysis & Reporting), ISC (Information Systems & Controls), or TCP (Tax Compliance & Planning). Each is 4 hours, with MCQs plus task-based simulations, scaled 0–99 (pass 75).

    • Core: AUD
    • Core: FAR
    • Core: REG
    • Discipline: BAR / ISC / TCP (choose one)
    ⏱ 6–12 months of testing Note: All 4 must be passed within an 18-month (most states) or 30-month window.
  5. 5

    Pass the AICPA Ethics Exam

    Most states require the AICPA self-study Ethics Exam — a 40-question, open-book test — before licensing. Cost is typically $150–$200.

    ⏱ A few weeks
  6. 6

    Complete the experience requirement

    Nearly every state requires 1–2 years (about 2,000 hours) of supervised accounting experience, verified by a licensed CPA.

    • Secure a CPA-supervised role
    • Log ~2,000 hours
    • Obtain CPA verification
    ⏱ 1–2 years Note: Some states accept industry or non-public accounting experience.
  7. 7

    Apply for your license

    Submit your final application and pay the license fee ($100–$500). Once issued, maintain it with ~120 hours of CPE every 3 years (varies by state).

    ⏱ 2–4 weeks

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the CPA Evolution?

A new CPA exam model introduced in 2024 featuring 3 mandatory Core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) and 1 of 3 chosen Discipline sections (BAR, ISC, TCP).

Summary

  • The CPA is the only license that lets you sign audit opinions and issue attestations.
  • The 150-hour education rule is the single biggest hurdle for most candidates.
  • Under CPA Evolution (2024+), you choose 1 of 3 Discipline sections after the 3 Core.
  • Most states give you 18 (some 30) months to pass all 4 sections once you pass your first.
