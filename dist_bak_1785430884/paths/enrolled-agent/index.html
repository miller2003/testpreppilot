IRS Enrolled Agent (EA)

An Enrolled Agent is a person who has earned the privilege of representing taxpayers before the Internal Revenue Service by passing a three-part comprehensive IRS test.

Exam Parts 3
Administering Body IRS (Prometric)
Quick Answer

An Enrolled Agent (EA) is a federally licensed tax practitioner who earns the credential by passing the three-part Special Enrollment Examination (SEE) or through IRS work experience. The fastest route is: get a PTIN, pass all three SEE parts, then file Form 23 to enroll — no college degree required.

  • No bachelor’s degree or 150 credit hours required (unlike the CPA)
  • Federally licensed — you can represent clients in any state, not just one
  • Lower total cost (~$1,100) than most accounting credentials
  • Testing windows are open year-round; you are not locked to certain months
  • Specialized, in-demand expertise in federal taxation

The Path at a Glance

  1. Get your PTIN (Preparer Tax Identification Number)
  2. Apply for and schedule the SEE (Special Enrollment Exam)
  3. Pass all 3 parts of the SEE
  4. Submit Form 23 and pass the IRS background check
  5. Maintain your license with 72 hours of CE every 3 years

Your Route

  1. 1

    Obtain your PTIN

    Every paid tax preparer must have a Preparer Tax Identification Number from the IRS. Create an account at IRS.gov and pay the annual fee (~$18.75 as of 2026). You cannot register for the SEE without one.

    ⏱ About 1 day Note: Your PTIN must stay active (renewed every year) throughout the process.
  2. 2

    Apply for the Special Enrollment Examination (SEE)

    Starting in 2026 the SEE is delivered by PSI (formerly Prometric). Create a PSI testing account, choose which part to take first, and pay the fee.

    • Create a PSI testing account
    • Choose Part 1, 2, or 3 to begin (order is your choice)
    • Pay $317 per part
    ⏱ Same day
  3. 3

    Pass all three parts of the SEE

    The SEE has three parts: Part 1 — Individuals; Part 2 — Businesses; Part 3 — Representation, Practices & Procedures. Each part is 100 multiple-choice questions in 3.5 hours, scored on a 500 scale (pass mark 105).

    • Part 1: Individuals
    • Part 2: Businesses
    • Part 3: Representation, Practices & Procedures
    ⏱ 3–6 months of study Note: Parts may be taken in any order; you have up to 3 years from your first pass to clear all three.
  4. 4

    Submit Form 23 and complete the IRS background check

    After passing all three parts, file Form 23 (Application for Enrollment to Practice Before the IRS) and pay the $140 fee. The IRS then runs a tax-compliance and background check, which takes about 60 days.

    ⏱ ~60 days
  5. 5

    Receive your enrollment and maintain it

    Once approved you are a federally authorized EA. To keep the license you must complete 72 hours of IRS-approved continuing education every 3 years, including at least 16 hours per year (2 of which are ethics).

    • Enroll with an IRS-approved CE provider
    • Complete 72 hours / 3 years (incl. 16 hrs/yr, 2 ethics)
    • Renew your PTIN annually

Frequently Asked Questions

EA vs CPA?

EAs specialize in taxation globally, CPAs have broader accounting scopes typically at a state level.

Summary

  • The Enrolled Agent is the only tax credential issued directly by the IRS, granting unlimited practice rights before the agency.
  • No degree is required; the entire process can be finished in roughly 6–12 months.
  • Total hard cost is about $1,100 for exams and enrollment.
  • Unlike a CPA, an EA’s license is portable across all 50 states.
